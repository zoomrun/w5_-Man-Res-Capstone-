The assignment

Make the learning visible in a real slice of ManRes work.

Each squad chooses one documented opportunity, frames a user problem, explores options, and creates a working prototype that makes the proposed outcome tangible.

01

Understand

Use journey, VSM, backlog, and product evidence. Separate facts from assumptions.

02

Decide

Choose one valuable problem and one smallest useful outcome. Make the trade-offs explicit.

03

Show

Build a working prototype and explain what you would measure next in Engage.

The goal is not to finish a production feature in two weeks. The goal is to demonstrate a coherent path from evidence to an outcome that can be learned from.

03 · Frame the opportunityOpportunity Canvas + execution add-ons
The guardrail

Label what you know, what you believe, and what you still need to learn.

Evidence

  • Observed in a source
  • Traceable to a journey, VSM, metric, or backlog record
  • Can be cited in the demo

Assumption

  • Believed to be true
  • Needs a reason or confidence level
  • Should influence the prototype or test

Unknown

  • Baseline not yet established
  • Dependency or policy still open
  • Becomes a next question, not a made-up fact
Example: “83% digital adoption” is a stated ambition in the evidence. It is not automatically the current baseline for every BE Award or servicing flow. The team must identify the relevant baseline or mark it as unknown.
04 · Protect the quality of the thinkingFacts are not guesses
The teach arc

Every block contributes one piece of the capstone.

Customer JourneyChoose a user, moment, pain, and desired outcome.
Measures + MetricsName the behavior to observe, its baseline, and the business connection.
Story Writing + DecompositionTurn the first outcome into a thin, testable slice with acceptance checks.
Incremental Delivery + Solution TreeCompare options, sequence learning, and build the prototype.

At the end of each teach block, spend 15 minutes updating the squad canvas. The artifact grows as the teaching happens; it is not a last-minute assignment.

08 · The finish linePrototype first, polish second
Teaching example · not a prescribed answer

Example: make the next self-service action clear.

Opportunity: Digitize customer journeys. This example shows the level of specificity expected; the numbers are intentionally not invented.

Canvas snapshot

Problem: A customer who has retrieved a reservation may not know the next self-service action available to them.

Customer: A customer managing an AA-issued trip through a digital channel.

Today: Verify the current journey and alternatives with users and existing flow data.

Business challenge: Unclear next steps can increase effort and channel switching.

Solution idea: A focused next-action experience for one selected servicing moment.

Prototype + measure

First slice: Walk one customer from reservation retrieval to one eligible next action.

Prototype: Clickable flow with a visible completion state and an alternate/error path.

Behavior metric: Completion of the selected self-service task.

Baseline: Not established in this teaching example; identify the source before claiming one.

Next learning: Can customers find and complete the action without switching channels?

This is a teaching example, not a claim that the current journey has this exact problem. The squad would validate the problem and metric before proposing delivery.

09 · See the shape of good workIllustrative example
The six-minute story

Make the demo easy to follow and hard to misunderstand.

Minutes 0–1

Context: Which opportunity and user problem did you choose? What evidence supports it?

Minutes 1–2

Decision: What outcome and first slice did you choose? What did you leave out?

Minutes 2–4

Demo: Show the working prototype through the happy path. Mention one boundary or error path.

Minutes 4–5

Measurement: What behavior would you observe? What is the baseline status? What is the business connection?

Minutes 5–6

Recommendation: What did you learn, what remains risky, and what should happen next in Engage?

End with a decision request, not a tour of every sticky note.

11 · Success guideClarity over presentation polish
Support model

Give the squads just enough structure to move.

Before the teach

Assign two squads to each opportunity. Create one Mural area per squad. Share the evidence pack and example canvas.

During the teach

Protect the 15-minute checkpoint. Ask “what changed?” and “what are we still assuming?” rather than solving for the squad.

On demo day

Timebox the six-minute demos. Leaders ask for the next decision: validate, refine, sequence, or stop.

Leader lens: Product asks whether the problem and outcome matter. Engineering asks whether the slice is buildable and observable. UX asks whether the experience helps the intended user.
